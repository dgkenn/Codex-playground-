# Niche-sport modeling edge — scan for a US small-bankroll bettor (2026-06-15)

Can a small bankroll beat the line in LESS-EFFICIENT sports via modeling? Synthesis of the niche-vig +
esports-softness research. Verdict up front: **real edge (~1.5–3.5% realized ROI) but a tiny-capacity,
high-effort grind that needs genuine modeling + data work — not a turnkey or scalable small-bankroll income.**

## Where the softness is (and the catch — vig scales inversely with liquidity)
| Niche | Vig/overround | Softness | Data | Net read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Esports** (CS2/LoL/Dota) | 6–10% (lower-tier up to 10%) | REAL at lower tiers + props/maps; books lift lines from shared vendors (Kambi/Oddin/DATA.BET) → correlated, beatable | free *stats* (GRID Open Access, bo3.gg) + free *odds* (OddsPapi); but PandaScore/Liquipedia BAN betting use, HLTV scraping breaks ToS | Best-documented niche; realized ROI **1.5–3.5%** (Arbusers), concentrated lower-tier; tightening |
| **Lower-division soccer** | ~7–7.6% (vs ~4.1–5.2% top flight) | Real (+2–3pts vs top flight); xG-modelable | free xG/Elo data exists | Modelable but higher vig eats most of it |
| **WNBA** | main lines ~2.3% (NOT soft) | main lines efficient; only props/attention soft | good free stats | Main lines too efficient; not the soft spot people assume |
| **Table tennis / darts / snooker** | higher (qualitative; exact % unpublished) | thin, high-frequency, low-liquidity | sparse | Soft but thinnest limits, fastest limiting |
| **KBO / NPB** | undocumented (baseball-structured) | overnight/thin | sparse | Unverified; thin |
| **Props / futures / parlays (any sport)** | **8–15% props, 15–30%+ futures/parlays** | softest lines BUT highest vig | — | Vig spike usually > the softness |

## The killers (why "real edge" ≠ "deployable income")
1. **Vig is HIGHER, not lower** — niche markets run 6–15% hold vs 2–5% on major spreads; the soft line must clear that wider hurdle first.
2. **Limits are TINY** ($50–$500 max bet on niche events) — this *both* caps capacity *and* is precisely why books tolerate the softness.
3. **Limiting is fastest** — niche-market winners get throttled within weeks (books watch low-liquidity books closely).
4. **It requires genuine modeling + data work** — a real Elo/xG/map-stats model, on data sources that often *prohibit betting use* (PandaScore/Liquipedia) → a friction-heavy free stack.
5. **CLV is a noisier benchmark in soft markets** (no sharp closing line to verify edge against), so you can't even cleanly prove you're +EV.

## Realistic economics
A competent modeler: ~2–5% ROI gross, ~1.5–3.5% realized (esports practitioner data). With $50–500 limits and
fast limiting, realistic extraction is **a few hundred $/month at most for a dedicated modeler**, decaying as
accounts get limited — and only after building a genuine model. It is a part-time skilled grind, not passive
or scalable.

## Trait scores (1–5, 5 favorable for small retail)
Recreational-flow **4** · ¬HFT **4** (slow, not latency) · fair-value/skill **3** (real model edge, but no clean
reference) · access **4** (US books, ~45 states) · ¬ban **2** (limited fastest of any bettor) · small-cap **3**

## VERDICT
**Real but not deployable as income for this operator.** The softness is genuine (esports lower-tier + props
the best spot), but higher vig + tiny limits + fast limiting + the need for real modeling/data make it a
low-capacity, high-effort grind yielding maybe a few hundred $/month, decaying. It does not beat the project's
core answer (the systematic risk-premium portfolio) and is dominated, as a sports play, by promo extraction
(SPORTS_PROMO_EV.md). Only worth pursuing by someone who genuinely enjoys building sport-specific models.
