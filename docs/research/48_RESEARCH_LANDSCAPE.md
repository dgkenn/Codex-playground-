# What could this project actually solve? A landscape assessment against the data we hold

*Written 2026-07-27, after R392. The question asked was: of the high-impact unsolved questions in EEG
research, which are tractable with our assets? This is the honest answer, with feasibility counts rather than
aspiration, and it names what is blocked as carefully as what is open.*

---

## TIER 1 — tractable, high impact, and we hold something others do not

### A. Are EEG prognostic criteria **aetiology-specific**?

ACNS and ERC-ESICM EEG criteria are derived overwhelmingly from **cardiac arrest** and applied across
aetiologies. R389–R392 shows at least one quantitative marker **reverses direction** by aetiology
(AUC 0.589 anoxic vs 0.408 non-anoxic, 4/4 non-anoxic subgroups agreeing within 0.028). If the categorical
findings behave the same way, the guidelines do not transfer — which is directly actionable.

Feasibility is not the constraint. Patients per finding × aetiology cell:

| finding | anoxic | sepsis | metabolic | structural | status | non-anoxic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| burst suppression | 1,363 | 1,280 | 2,271 | 1,575 | 596 | 1,717 |
| GPD | 649 | 1,194 | 1,829 | 1,251 | 389 | 1,648 |
| LPD | 226 | 634 | 1,062 | 1,170 | 447 | 1,405 |
| seizure | 822 | 1,789 | 3,276 | 3,097 | 845 | 3,941 |
| generalized slowing | 1,720 | 4,310 | 8,222 | 7,045 | 1,110 | 9,727 |

**Why we can do this and most cannot:** a single-aetiology cohort cannot test an aetiology contrast at all.
I-CARE is entirely cardiac arrest and is structurally incapable of it. This is the clearest case where our
data is the differentiating asset rather than our method.

### B. The thalamocortical hypothesis, tested with **sleep spindles** instead of a vague flag

Spindles are generated by the **thalamic reticular nucleus** — an anatomically specific readout of
thalamocortical integrity, and a far better instrument than "generalized slowing", which we now know is
83.8 % prevalent and strongly burden-dependent. R389 found the slowing flag's effect is larger after anoxia
(interaction −0.750 [−1.433, −0.116]); the thalamocortical account predicts the **same pattern, more sharply,
for spindles**.

Raw feasibility (n = 15,857 with an ascertained death and an EEG time):

| marker | prevalence | n | 30-day death present vs absent | anoxic only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| spindles | 43.5 % | 6,891 | 21.5 % vs 36.1 % | 43.5 % vs 66.2 % |
| N2 | 56.2 % | 8,911 | 21.2 % vs 40.7 % | 42.1 % vs 69.5 % |
| N1 | 59.0 % | 9,353 | 15.2 % vs 50.7 % | 22.7 % vs 73.9 % |
| vertex waves | 29.5 % | 4,680 | 14.1 % vs 36.3 % | 29.7 % vs 64.4 % |
| POSTS | 3.3 % | 528 | 8.0 % vs 30.5 % | 4.5 % vs 58.8 % |

**THE CONFOUND, and it is severe enough to sink a naive analysis.** Sleep architecture requires a patient
who cycles. A comatose patient has no spindles by definition, so "spindles present" is substantially "not
deeply encephalopathic", and the raw contrasts above are close to meaningless on their own. The question is
whether spindles carry information **beyond depth** — burden, background, and the awake flag — and whether
that residual is **aetiology-dependent**. That is exactly the adjustment discipline already built and
calibrated here (R369–R370, R388).

**Why it is worth doing anyway:** it converts our mechanism from a story into an anatomically specific
prediction, it connects to Schiff's mesocircuit framework (PMID 31994749) and to Brown's own work on
dexmedetomidine spindle generation, and no prior work tests spindle preservation against outcome at this
scale in a mixed-aetiology critical-care population.

### C. Distinct burst-suppression phenotypes: anaesthetic versus pathological

This is **a published call from the target reader's own group** — Guay et al./Brown, *Anesthesiology* 2025;
143(6):1595-1618, "characterize distinct burst suppression phenotypes". We hold all three regimes:
**VitalDB** (anaesthetic, reversible — ICC 0.313, 70 % resolution), **HEEDB** (pathological, mixed
aetiology), **I-CARE** (pathological, pure anoxic). Our own R389–R392 already argues burst suppression is not
one thing.

## TIER 2 — tractable, lower ceiling

**D. Pharmaco-EEG at population scale.** `drug_exposure` joined to EEG: what each sedative does,
dose-dependently, in a real ICU population rather than a volunteer study. Less confounded than outcome work
because drug → EEG is a direct pharmacological effect. Solid, useful, not headline.

**E. What clinical EEG reports actually capture.** We found the generalized-slowing flag is 83.8 % prevalent
and its positivity collapses from 92.7 % to 56.1 % across the burden range — readers describe a suppressed
record as suppressed, not slow. A systematic report-versus-quantitative study at 49k scale, with MORGOTH
expert labels as a partial reference, is genuinely useful measurement science with a modest ceiling.

## TIER 3 — high impact, and **blocked** by limits no amount of data fixes

**F. Does treating the ictal-interictal continuum improve outcomes?** Probably the biggest open question in
acute EEG, and MORGOTH supplies the IIIC labels. **Blocked:** confounding by indication is the precise
failure mode that has defeated this project four separate times (N14 — four instruments, one root cause), and
we have no instrument for treatment assignment. Attempting it without one would produce a confidently wrong
answer.

**G′. NOTE (R398): one structural limit turned out to be self-inflicted.** L3 — every patient having an
ascertained death — bounded every aetiology analysis here and was an artefact of which patient list a prior
OMOP extraction targeted, not a property of HEEDB. Re-extracting took sixteen minutes and added 953
survivors. **Before treating any limit in this document as structural, check whether it is an extraction
choice.** The remaining limits below have been checked and are not.

**G. Anything requiring separation of withdrawal from biological death.** **L2**: 46 % die inside the
withdrawal window. This is a design limitation, not a sample-size one.

**H. Anything requiring EEG reactivity.** **L5**: not recorded in this schema.

## The recommendation

**A and B together are one paper, and it is the strongest available.** A supplies the clinical claim — EEG
prognostic markers are aetiology-specific, so criteria derived from cardiac arrest should not be applied
uniformly. B supplies the mechanism — thalamocortical integrity, measured by an anatomically specific marker,
is what differs. Each is weak alone: A without a mechanism is a curiosity, B without the clinical contrast is
a physiology note. Together they are a claim with a reason.

**The single biggest threat to both is external replication**, and it has no easy answer. I-CARE cannot
help, being single-aetiology. **TUH cannot help either — corrected 2026-07-27:** it carries no outcome data
and no diagnosis (ledger R321), so it can validate a measurement but never an outcome association. No known
cohort has EEG + outcome + mixed aetiology other than HEEDB itself. The strongest available substitute is the
**HEEDB hospital split (S0001 vs S0002)**, and it should be stated as the internal validation it is.
