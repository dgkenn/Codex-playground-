## ISSUE 1 — CRITICAL — NEW

### The control-theory mechanism (VitalDB) and the mortality signal (MIMIC) refer to STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT physiological objects, and fusing them under the label "vasopressor requirement is a trait" commits an estimand seam error

**What the unified claim asserts.** The paper presents a single conceptual object — "the vasopressor dose-requirement" — grounded in the control-theory narrative (MAP is feedback-regulated, so dose encodes the hemodynamic insult), demonstrated mechanistically in VitalDB (MAP CV 0.09 vs dose CV 0.44), and confirmed as a mortality-graded trait in MIMIC (landmark OR 2.27). The prospective endorsement (OR 2.27 [2.10,2.48]) is carried by the MIMIC landmark. The mechanism that JUSTIFIES calling the MIMIC median dose a "requirement" rather than a "severity correlate" is the VitalDB control-theory observation.

**The structural problem.** The VitalDB "requirement" is: median norepinephrine rate (mL/h/kg, device units) during stable MAP-band epochs [55–80 mmHg], norepi-only, 60-second settle time, with simultaneous MAP documented. The within-patient MAP CV 0.09 vs dose CV 0.44 is computed WITHIN these MAP-conditioned stable epochs. This is genuinely a "controller effort at constant set-point" measurement.

The MIMIC "requirement" is: median norepinephrine rate (mcg/kg/min) across ALL infusion segments within an ICU stay, with no MAP conditioning, no epoch stability requirement, and no MAP data pulled at all (MIMIC_EXTERNAL_VALIDATION.md explicitly states: "MIMIC rate is already per-kg; MIMIC pulls no MAP"). The landmark restricts to first-24h NEE but still makes no MAP conditioning.

These are not two measurements of the same quantity. The VitalDB object controls for the MAP at which the dose is delivered; the MIMIC object is a MAP-unconditional dose rate. The control-theory argument for why dose > MAP as a severity signal applies ONLY to the MAP-conditioned quantity: if you don't hold MAP constant, a patient requiring 0.4 mcg/kg/min at MAP 65 and a patient requiring 0.4 mcg/kg/min at MAP 55 (despite same dose) are not in the same physiological state — the latter patient has a worse hemodynamic phenotype for the same dose, but the MIMIC metric assigns them identical "requirement" values.

**Why the existing disclosure is insufficient.** The dossier acknowledges this as "two-level replication — VitalDB and MIMIC are different estimands; dose-ordering replicates, not the MAP-conditioned phenotype/mechanism." But the framing of the unified claim still presents the control-theory motivation as the causal anchor for the MIMIC mortality finding. A reader — and a sophisticated reviewer — will ask: if the MIMIC quantity is not the MAP-conditioned controller effort, what is the mechanistic justification for interpreting the MIMIC median dose as a "requirement" trait rather than merely as a severity proxy? The answer is: the MIMIC metric IS a severity proxy, and the MIMIC mortality OR is a severity-graded association, which is precisely what confounding-by-indication means. The control-theory narrative does not save the MIMIC finding from this; it only saves the VitalDB finding.

**The deeper problem for the prospective claim.** The MIMIC landmark OR 2.27 (the paper's strongest prospective number) is generated by the MAP-unconditional MIMIC metric. The argument that this OR represents a "trait" rather than a "severity surrogate" depends on the control-theory mechanism — but that mechanism is not validated in MIMIC. The circular structure is: we call it a "requirement" (not a severity proxy) because the control-theory premise justifies this labeling; but the control-theory premise is only shown where the outcome (MAP-conditioned design) forces it to hold (VitalDB). In MIMIC, without MAP conditioning, we have no empirical basis to distinguish "high dose needed for the same hemodynamic goal" from "high dose because the patient is profoundly shocked and the clinician is trying to raise an inadequate MAP." These are different physiological states that the MIMIC metric conflates.

**Quantitative test that is missing.** A MAP-stratified analysis in MIMIC (if MAP were pulled from chartevents) would allow the same MAP-conditioning as VitalDB and would test whether the MIMIC signal persists WITHIN MAP strata (analogous to the control-theory argument). Without this, the mechanism the paper invokes to elevate "dose" above "severity surrogate" is not demonstrated for the quantity carrying the mortality result.

**The fix / required disclosure.** Option A (preferred): Explicitly state in the abstract and results that the MIMIC mortality finding is grounded in an MAP-unconditional dose metric, that the control-theory mechanism is demonstrated only in VitalDB, and that the MIMIC landmark OR 2.27 should be read as a risk-stratification association without mechanistic claim. The "requirement" framing in the MIMIC context should be hedged as "dose-ordering, a proxy for requirement." Option B (analysis): Pull MAP from MIMIC chartevents, compute MAP CV for a representative pressor-exposed subsample, and test whether the MAP CV << dose CV signature replicates. If it does, the mechanism is supported. If MAP CV is comparable to dose CV in the ICU (plausible, since ICU vasopressor titration is less tightly regulated to a point target than intraoperative anesthesia), the control-theory argument collapses for the ICU setting and the MIMIC OR stands only as an observational association.

**Why CRITICAL.** The control-theory framing is the paper's primary novelty claim. The MIMIC landmark OR is the paper's primary prospective number. Connecting them requires the mechanism to hold in MIMIC. It is not demonstrated there. A methodologically sophisticated reviewer will identify this seam and write a rejection citing unjustified mechanistic overclaim. The prior rounds have disclosed the "different estimands" limitation but have NOT identified the specific logical error: that the mechanism justifying the "requirement" label applies to VitalDB but not to the MIMIC quantity that carries the prospective result. This is a NEW hole, not merely the previously disclosed "two-level replication" limitation.

## ISSUE 2 — CRITICAL — NEW

### The propofol negative-control is not a valid negative-control exposure for confounding-by-indication because propofol and norepinephrine are administered in non-overlapping severity strata within the ventilated population, invalidating the head-to-head comparison

**The claim being tested.** In the head-to-head model (death ~ norepi + propofol + age, n=9,203), norepi OR 3.01 [2.74,3.30] vs propofol OR 0.88 [0.83,0.93]. The argument: if the vasopressor signal were "generic treatment intensity" (sicker patients get more of everything), propofol would also predict death. Propofol does not; therefore the vasopressor requirement is vasopressor-specific, not generic.

**The structural problem.** A valid negative-control exposure must satisfy three conditions:
1. It must be titrated to the SAME severity signal as the index exposure (norepinephrine).
2. It must NOT have a causal pathway to the outcome (here, mortality) through the mechanism being tested (vasopressor requirement → hemodynamic insufficiency → death).
3. The relationship between the negative control and the index exposure must not introduce collider bias in the conditioning set.

**Condition 1 is violated.** Propofol in the ICU is titrated to sedation depth (RASS or SAS target), not to hemodynamic severity. Norepinephrine is titrated to MAP. These are different clinical control loops with different target variables. A sicker patient (say, septic shock with lactate 8) will receive high norepinephrine AND — if intubated — some propofol, but the dose of propofol tracks the anesthetic state (RASS) not the hemodynamic state (MAP). When you condition on norepinephrine, propofol carries the residual signal of "mechanically ventilated + not too sick to tolerate sedation." The propofol OR < 1 may reflect that patients who get high-propofol doses are SURVIVING long enough to require ongoing sedation — a survivorship/immortal-time artifact — rather than a true null effect on the severity pathway.

**Condition 3 is violated (collider bias).** The n=9,203 analysis is restricted to patients who received BOTH norepi AND propofol. This is a conditioning on a collider: both norepi use and propofol use are downstream of ICU admission severity. By restricting to the co-exposed subset, you create a selected population where the bivariate distribution of (norepi, propofol) is constrained. Within this subset, high propofol may index "hemodynamically stable enough to sedate" which is NEGATIVELY correlated with high norepinephrine requirement even in the absence of any causal effect of propofol on mortality. The apparent protection from propofol (OR 0.88) could be an artifact of this selection.

**The dossier's own caveat (CONFOUNDING_QUASI_EXPERIMENT.md line 33) states:** "the negative control is imperfect: propofol use itself tracks mechanical ventilation and severity, so a non-null propofol OR is expected; the argument rests on the SPECIFICITY gap (norepi >> propofol after mutual adjustment), not on propofol being exactly null." This caveat acknowledges the problem but does not quantify its magnitude or offer a fix. The prior red-team (REDTEAM_PUB_FINDING1.md, M4) called for restricting the analysis to mechanically ventilated patients only. This was NOT done. Critically, even within mechanically ventilated patients, the collider problem persists: the co-exposed restriction (norepi + propofol) is still a collider selection.

**What the correct negative-control analysis would require.** A valid negative control for "sicker patients get more of everything" should use an exposure that: (a) is given to nearly all ICU patients (not co-exposed-only), (b) is titrated to a severity signal that tracks the same latent variable as norepinephrine dose, but (c) has no vasopressor-specific pathway to death. A benchmark drug titrated to symptom rather than hemodynamics (e.g., opioid dose for pain — itself not without confounders, but more plausible than propofol) or a dose metric that is truly administrative (IV fluid volume per hour, which is protocolized and less severity-responsive) would be more defensible.

**The specific error in the existing argument.** The prior round concluded: "propofol OR 0.88 is weak/null vs norepi OR 3.798 (ratio 4.58); the vasopressor requirement is SPECIFIC, not generic treatment intensity." This conclusion requires that propofol and norepinephrine are equally well-positioned to detect "generic treatment intensity." They are not: norepinephrine dose has a direct hemodynamic pathway to mortality (through its role as a marker of refractory shock); propofol dose does not have a plausible direct pathway. The comparison is therefore between a specific-pathway exposure and a non-pathway exposure — of course they produce different ORs. The negative-control interpretation requires that in the absence of a SPECIFIC pathway, the generic intensity confounding would produce a similar OR for propofol and norepi. But the co-exposed restriction and the different titration targets mean this counterfactual is not tested. The OR 0.88 for propofol is MORE CONSISTENT with survivorship bias in the co-exposed subset than with absence of generic intensity confounding.

**The severity E-value remains the stronger argument.** The E-value ~6 (and CI E-value ~5.5) is a more robust argument against confounding-by-indication than the propofol negative-control. If the paper's confounding-by-indication argument is re-anchored on: (1) E-value ~6, (2) 8/8 within-severity strata, (3) homogeneous-indication restriction — without leaning on the propofol negative-control as quasi-experimental — the argument is more defensible, though narrower.

**The fix / required disclosure.** Demote the propofol negative-control from "quasi-experimental check" to "exploratory specificity analysis." Clearly state that the propofol comparison is restricted to the co-exposed subset (a collider restriction) and that propofol is titrated to a different clinical target than norepinephrine, invalidating a strict negative-control interpretation. The main confounding-by-indication argument should rest on E-value + within-severity strata + homogeneous-indication restriction. A better negative-control (opioid infusion dose, fluid administration rate in the first 6h) could be attempted, with explicit discussion of its own limitations.

**Why CRITICAL.** The prior round elevated the propofol negative-control to one of the "five fronts" against confounding-by-indication, and REDTEAM_PUB_FINDING1.md's M4 called for a restricted ventilated-only analysis that was not completed. A reviewer who understands the collider-in-co-exposed-restriction problem (a well-documented DAG error in observational ICU studies) will reject the quasi-experimental framing. This is a NEW structural critique: the prior round identified that propofol tracks ventilation (partially true) and that the analysis should be restricted to ventilated patients (a partial fix), but did NOT identify the collider selection problem, which persists even within ventilated patients and invalidates the strict negative-control interpretation.

## ISSUE 3 — CRITICAL — NEW

### The landmark test defeats reverse-causation but the surviving OR 2.27 faces an unaddressed severity-omission problem: MAP at 24h and vasopressor dose at 24h are deterministically linked, so first-24h NEE indexes "current hemodynamic severity" not "trait," and the landmark severity adjustment (age + lactate) is insufficient for this specific confounding structure

**What the landmark establishes.** The FINDING4_LANDMARK.md test: first-24h NEE, patients alive at 24h (n=23,925; 1,194 excluded for dying before 24h landmark), predicts subsequent in-hospital death. Age-adj OR 2.57 [2.45,2.68]; age+lactate OR 2.27 [2.10,2.48]. The tautology/reverse-causation attack (dying patients accumulate dose) is rejected by the temporal ordering.

**The new residual confounding problem.** The first-24h NEE in the MIMIC ICU is not an inert baseline characteristic measured before disease onset; it is itself a dynamic severity signal integrated over 24 hours of active ICU management. Patients with high first-24h NEE are, by definition, patients with high vasopressor requirements IN THE FIRST 24 HOURS — which is itself a well-validated severity marker (septic shock by vasopressor criteria in Sepsis-3 requires a MAP-corrected vasopressor), a component of SOFA score (SOFA cardiovascular subcomponent is determined by vasopressor dose), and a predictor of subsequent organ failure.

The specific confounding structure here is: first-24h NEE load → subsequent mortality, where first-24h NEE is a function of [intrinsic vascular phenotype + admission severity + ICU trajectory in first 24h]. The severity adjustment used is age + lactate. Lactate at 24h is approximately adjusted for here (MIMIC_SOFA_LACTATE.md: "first-24h worst labs"), but the critical missing variable is: **SOFA cardiovascular subcomponent at 24 hours** (which is directly defined by vasopressor dose tiers — norepinephrine/epinephrine/dopamine/vasopressin), **admission APACHE-II or SAPS-II score** (severity at ICU entry), and **first-24h MAP trajectory** (whether the patient's MAP was maintained or continued to drift despite vasopressors — a direct marker of refractory shock severity).

**The circular trap.** The SOFA cardiovascular score at 24h is a categorical function of the vasopressor dose at 24h. Adjusting for SOFA cardiovascular while keeping NEE as the exposure induces near-perfect collinearity. However, NOT adjusting for it leaves a major confounder unaddressed. The correct approach is: adjust for SOFA non-cardiovascular components (neurological, respiratory, renal, coagulation, hepatic) + admission severity (APACHE-II at admission, before the dose is given) while keeping the NEE as the exposure. This would separate "baseline severity" from "trajectory/phenotype." The current adjustment (age + first-24h worst lactate) captures only a 1-dimensional severity proxy and does not account for:
  - Respiratory failure severity (PaO2/FiO2 ratio, which requires chartevents)
  - Neurological status at admission (GCS, which requires chartevents)
  - Admission-to-ICU timing and source (ED vs surgical vs step-down — different severity distributions)
  - Fluid resuscitation volume in the first 24h (which affects how much vasopressor is needed through preload-responsiveness)

**The missing severity variable that is MOST important.** The strongest predictor of subsequent death in a septic shock patient who is alive at 24h is whether their vasopressor requirement is INCREASING or DECREASING over the first 24h (trajectory) rather than the absolute 24h NEE. A patient who starts at 0.5 mcg/kg/min and is weaned to 0.1 mcg/kg/min by 24h has a different prognosis than a patient who starts at 0.1 and escalates to 0.5 — yet both may have similar total 24h NEE. The landmark design uses cumulative NEE, which cannot separate these trajectories. The trajectory variable is not adjusted for and is correlated with both NEE and mortality.

**Why this is NEW.** Prior rounds examined the SOFA approximation as a limitation (no GCS, no PaO2/FiO2 for the SOFA labs model in the full cohort). But the landmark analysis (the strongest Finding 4 result) has ONLY age+lactate adjustment. The prior-round disclosure focused on "SOFA approximated by lab components + #vasopressors; no GCS/PaO2/FiO2" in the context of the full-cohort OR (~2.5 vs full SOFA), not in the context of the landmark-specific adjustment. The landmark-specific adjustment gap (absent admission-severity scoring; absent vasopressor trajectory; absent respiratory SOFA; absent 24h MAP) is a substantially larger problem for the OR 2.27 than the prior round disclosed. The landmark age+lactate OR 2.27 is the paper's PRIMARY prospective number, and it has the THINNEST severity adjustment of any major result. This is the inverse of what the adjustment hierarchy should look like: the result most vulnerable to severity confounding (prospective prediction from a first-24h severity-correlated metric) has the least adjustment.

**Quantitative indication of the problem.** In the full cohort (Finding 1, MIMIC), adding lactate + SOFA labs drops the OR from ~3.8 (age-only) to 2.5 (full), a 34% attenuation. If a similar proportional attenuation applies to the landmark OR 2.57 (age-only), a full severity model might yield OR ~1.7–2.0. The landmark + full severity OR is unknown and has NOT been computed. The OR 2.27 is age+lactate only; if the SOFA lab components and admission scoring are added, the OR could attenuate further. The paper's claim that the landmark "defeats the tautology" is correct, but the paper's implicit claim that OR 2.27 (age+lactate) represents the "beyond-severity" residual is not substantiated without a full severity model in the landmark.

**The fix / required disclosure.** (1) Run the landmark logistic regression adding Charlson/Elixhauser (available from diagnosis codes, same as the full cohort), and the full lab-severity panel (creatinine, bilirubin, platelets, WBC, bicarbonate, BUN, albumin — the same panel used in MIMIC_SOFA_LACTATE.md) restricted to first-24h values. (2) Report the resulting landmark+full-severity OR and its CI. If it stays well above 1.0 (say, OR > 1.5), the prospective signal is robust. If it attenuates to near 1.0, the OR 2.27 is attributable to incomplete severity adjustment rather than a true "beyond-severity" trait. (3) In any case, the current disclosure should explicitly state that the landmark OR 2.27 is adjusted for age and lactate only, that a full severity model has not been computed for the landmark, and that additional attenuation is possible.

**Why CRITICAL.** The landmark OR 2.27 [2.10,2.48] is the paper's single strongest prospective claim ("PROSPECTIVE LANDMARK OR 2.27" in the central claim sentence). The uncertainty about how much this OR would attenuate under full severity adjustment is not disclosed in the prospective-result framing. This is not a question that can be answered by gesturing at the full-cohort OR attenuation; the landmark population (alive at 24h, first 24h of vasopressor exposure) has a different severity distribution than the full stay population.

## ISSUE 4 — MODERATE — NEW

### The arterial-line / already-on-pressor selection denominator is a compound selection bias that may inflate both reliability and mortality association via two independent mechanisms

**What is acknowledged.** HOSTILE_REVIEW_FINAL.md disclosure #4: "Selection — arterial-line / already-on-pressor denominator; generalizes to that population." PUBLICATION_DOSSIER.md Finding 1 limits: "selection (arterial-line/on-pressor)."

**The NEW argument: collider bias INTO the trait measurement.** The VitalDB phenotype requires: (a) norepinephrine infusion, (b) arterial line, (c) ≥2 stable MAP-band epochs. The MIMIC cohort requires: (d) norepinephrine infusion. Both selections condition on BEING ON VASOPRESSORS — a variable that is downstream of both the underlying vascular phenotype (the exposure of interest) AND of hemodynamic severity (the confounder). This creates a collider on the causal DAG:

`Vascular phenotype → [vasopressor use] ← Hemodynamic severity`

Conditioning on "vasopressor use" (the selection criterion) opens the backdoor path: within the selected population, patients with LOW intrinsic vascular phenotype severity who nonetheless require vasopressors must have HIGH "other severity" to explain their vasopressor use, and vice versa. This produces a spurious negative correlation between the vascular phenotype and other severity indicators WITHIN the selected sample. Concretely: within the pressor-exposed ICU population, patients who require high vasopressor doses for a given level of measured severity (lactate/SOFA) look like they have a "unique" vascular requirement — but this uniqueness is partly manufactured by the collider conditioning.

**How this affects the key results.** (1) The within-severity-stratum ORs (8/8 > 1) are computed WITHIN the pressor-exposed population. The collider selection means the dose-requirement and the measured severity scores are negatively correlated within this sample (collider stratification bias), which can INFLATE the apparent OR within strata. Patients who need high doses at low measured severity are partially indexed by collider selection, not purely by vascular phenotype. (2) The reliability (0.82–0.95) is measured within the pressor-exposed subset. Any trait that is a selection criterion exhibits higher within-subset consistency than if measured in an unselected population (restriction-range effects combined with collider bias inflate the apparent reliability). (3) The E-value argument is computed within the selected population; the counterfactual comparison (a confounder strong enough to explain the association) should account for the fact that the background severity distribution is already conditional on vasopressor use, compressing the within-sample confounding magnitude.

**Why this is NEW relative to existing disclosure.** The existing disclosure treats the selection as a scope/generalizability issue: "generalizes to that population." The NEW argument is that conditioning on vasopressor use as a selection criterion introduces COLLIDER BIAS that can inflate the within-sample dose-requirement → mortality association independently of true confounding-by-indication, and can inflate the apparent reliability of the dose-requirement trait. This is not a generalizability limitation; it is a validity threat specific to within-sample estimates. The prior rounds did not apply DAG analysis to the selection criterion.

**Quantitative illustration of the collider mechanism.** If the unselected ICU population has 40% on vasopressors, the selected sample (40% of the original) has, by construction, higher mean severity. Within this selected sample, the severity-dose correlation is distorted: a patient with lactate 2 who is on vasopressors has a different "why" than a patient with lactate 8 who is on vasopressors, and the distribution of unmeasured confounders is systematically different between these two. Any analysis that adjusts for lactate within the pressor-exposed sample is adjusting in a collider-selected population where the residual confounding is NOT the same as in the unselected population. The E-value applies to an unselected population; the E-value computed within the selected sample is not the same quantity.

**The fix / required disclosure.** (1) Add to the paper's limitations section an explicit statement that all within-sample analyses (within-severity strata, reliability, E-value) are computed in a vasopressor-conditioned sample, that this conditioning opens a collider path, and that the E-value and within-stratum ORs may be optimistic within this selected sample. (2) As a sensitivity analysis: reframe the MIMIC analysis as a two-stage model — first stage predicts vasopressor use (propensity); second stage tests the dose-requirement signal within the propensity-overlap subset. If the OR persists after propensity-based exclusion of highly-selected patients, the collider concern is partially addressed. (3) The reliability estimates should note that split-half reliability within a vasopressor-conditioned sample may overstate the population-level reliability.

## ISSUE 5 — MODERATE — ALREADY-DISCLOSED (BUT UNDERSTATED)

### The "risk-stratification not causal" scope does not fully neutralize the control-theory framing, which is a mechanistic claim that the data cannot support even for risk-stratification purposes

**What is disclosed.** All documents: "observational; the claim stays risk-stratification, not a treatment effect or practice-changer."

**Why the disclosure is insufficient.** "Control theory" is a mechanistic claim: the MAP is feedback-regulated; therefore the dose encodes the hemodynamic insult; therefore the dose is a better marker than the MAP. This chain of reasoning is causal in structure, not merely descriptive. Even scoped to risk-stratification, using the control-theory mechanism to argue that "dose is better than pressure" requires establishing: (i) that MAP is actually regulated to a constant set-point (approximately true intraoperatively, contested in the ICU), and (ii) that the dose is the controller effort in a linear control system (not true — clinicians titrate in discrete steps, there are medication delays, and the dose-to-MAP transfer function is nonlinear in vasoplegic patients). The VitalDB data show that MAP CV << dose CV, which is consistent with the control-theory story but does not prove that clinicians are operating as linear feedback controllers. Alternative explanations include: (a) MAP is stabilized by all homeostatic mechanisms (autogenic + pharmacologic), while dose is determined solely by the clinician's judgment — a more complex causal structure; (b) dose CV >> MAP CV because clinical dosing is noisy and discretized, while MAP is a smoother physiological variable — this is a measurement artefact, not a control-theory signature; (c) within-patient dose variation reflects clinician UNCERTAINTY rather than a tight control loop — uncertainty being proportional to the severity of the underlying phenotype.

**Why this is an estimand problem for the prospective MIMIC claim.** The prospective claim (OR 2.27) is offered as confirmation that the "requirement" is a patient trait that predicts future death. But if the control-theory mechanism doesn't apply to the ICU setting, the "requirement" is simply a cumulative severity measure — informative for risk-stratification but interpretable by its predictive value alone, not by the mechanistic framing. The problem is that the prospective claim's magnitude (OR 2.27 after age+lactate) is used to argue that the requirement adds information BEYOND severity — and this "beyond severity" argument is precisely what the control-theory mechanism is invoked to explain. Without the mechanism, the interpretation reverts to: "higher vasopressor dose predicts death beyond lactate, in patients already selected to be on vasopressors." This is a narrower and less novel claim.

**Severity:** MODERATE. The existing "risk-stratification not causal" scope partially addresses this, but does not honestly confront that the control-theory framing is making an implicit mechanistic claim that the data cannot support in the MIMIC setting. The fix is the same as Issue 1 Option A: explicitly decouple the control-theory motivation (VitalDB, hypothesis-generating) from the MIMIC mortality association (purely descriptive risk-stratification, no mechanistic claim).

**Status:** ALREADY-DISCLOSED at a superficial level ("single-mechanism setting — control-theory premise shown in VitalDB only"), but the implication — that the prospective MIMIC claim cannot be interpreted as "beyond-severity due to the control-theory mechanism" — is NOT stated explicitly. The fix is a single sentence in the paper: "We caution that the control-theory interpretation (MAP-conditioned controller effort) applies to the VitalDB intraoperative phenotype; the MIMIC ICU metric is an MAP-unconditional dose rate, and the prospective association should be interpreted as an observational risk-stratification signal without mechanistic grounding."

## ISSUE 6 — MODERATE — NEW

### The E-value is computed on the Q4/Q1 RR 3.27 from the severity-adjusted dose-response, but the E-value interpretation is applied to argue against confounding for the LANDMARK OR 2.27 — these are different effect sizes in different populations with different adjustments, and the E-value cannot be transported across them

**The E-value claim.** "E-value ~6 (CI lower ~5.5): an unmeasured confounder would need RR >= 5.5-6 with BOTH requirement and death, beyond age+Charlson+Elixhauser+#vaso, to nullify the adjusted dose-response." (CONFOUNDING_BY_INDICATION.md)

**The problem.** The E-value of 5.99 is computed from the Q4/Q1 RR 3.27 in the full MIMIC cohort severity-adjusted for Charlson + Elixhauser + #vasopressors. The primary prospective result that the paper foregrounds is the LANDMARK OR 2.27 (age+lactate only, alive-at-24h subsample, first-24h NEE). The landmark result has:
- A different population (alive at 24h, n=23,925 vs n=15,949)
- A different exposure (first-24h NEE for all vasopressors vs per-stay median norepi rate)
- A different adjustment set (age+lactate only vs full severity)
- A different E-value: from OR 2.27, approximate RR ~1.65, E-value ~2.65 (E-value = RR + sqrt(RR*(RR-1)) ~ 1.65 + sqrt(1.65*0.65) ~ 1.65 + 1.04 = 2.69)

The E-value for the landmark primary result is approximately 2.7, not 6. An E-value of 2.7 means an unmeasured confounder with RR 2.7 for both requirement and death would suffice to explain the association — this is a substantially weaker claim than "RR >= 5.5-6." Given that admission severity scoring (APACHE-II, SAPS-II), which is likely correlated with first-24h NEE at RR ~2–3, is not included in the landmark model, the E-value threshold is not implausible to exceed. The paper cannot present E-value ~6 as the relevant threshold for the landmark result.

**Why NEW.** The prior rounds computed the E-value in the context of the full-cohort Q4/Q1 dose-response and did not recompute it for the landmark. The landmark is a different analysis with a different (smaller) effect size and different adjustment set. The E-value is effect-size-specific; transporting E-value ~6 to argue against confounding for OR 2.27 is an error.

**The fix.** Compute the E-value for the landmark OR 2.27 specifically (approximately 2.69 as above). State it explicitly. Acknowledge that this is a lower threshold than the full-cohort E-value and is potentially achievable by incomplete severity adjustment. The confounding-by-indication argument for the landmark OR 2.27 is weaker than for the full-cohort OR 3.27 by this criterion.

## ISSUE 7 — MINOR — ALREADY-DISCLOSED

### The first-6h early-warning OR 1.54 vs characterization OR ~3.8 conflation risk in the central claim sentence

**The central claim.** "Prospective landmark OR 2.27 [2.10,2.48] (MIMIC)." This is the 24h landmark. The genuinely earliest-warning prospective signal is the first-6h OR 1.54 (MIMIC_EARLY_WARNING.md). The 24h landmark uses 24h of data; the 6h landmark uses 6h. Both are prospective, but they have different clinical meanings: OR 1.54 is what you can predict at 6h; OR 2.27 is what you can predict at 24h (with 4x more dose information). The paper presents OR 2.27 as the key "beyond-severity" prospective number without performing the beyond-severity test for this specific population.
