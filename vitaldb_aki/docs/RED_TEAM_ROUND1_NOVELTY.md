## Preamble: the claim under review

> "The early vasopressor REQUIREMENT is a reliable, prospectively-validated,
> mortality-graded patient trait — a CONTROL-THEORY reframing of perioperative
> hemodynamic risk: intraoperative arterial pressure is feedback-regulated
> (clinicians titrate pressor to hold MAP at target), so the hemodynamic insult
> is carried by the DOSE/requirement, not the regulated pressure (MAP CV 0.09 <<
> dose CV 0.44, VitalDB). The per-kg requirement is a reliable trait
> (0.82/0.87/0.95), grades mortality beyond severity (OR 2.4–2.5), and
> prospectively (landmark OR 2.27). Risk-stratification, not a decision tool."

Searches conducted on PubMed (search date: 2026-06-30) covering: vasopressor dose
mortality perioperative; vasoactive-inotropic score (VIS) mortality; norepinephrine
equivalents vasopressor dependency mortality; closed-loop vasopressor anesthesia;
vasopressor requirement phenotype reliability; intraoperative cumulative vasopressor
dose outcome; vasopressor dose-response mortality sepsis.

## 1. NOVELTY / PRIOR ART

### 1A. What IS already well-established in the literature — CRITICAL

**VIS (Vasoactive-Inotropic Score) — directly analogous construct, extensively
validated.**

The VIS (dopamine + dobutamine + 100×epinephrine + 100×norepinephrine + 10,000×
vasopressin + 10×milrinone, in µg/kg/min) was developed specifically to quantify
the aggregate vasopressor/inotropic load and has been validated as a mortality
predictor in cardiac surgery, transplantation, ECMO, neonatal cardiac surgery, and
neonatal septic shock — across at least 58 studies (29,920 patients) in a 2024
meta-analysis (Sun et al., Syst Rev 2024; DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-023-02403-1). Key results from PubMed:

- Koponen et al., BJA 2019 (DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2018.12.019):
  VIS in first 24h post cardiac surgery (n=3,213) predicts 30-day mortality
  (AUC 0.76), 1-yr mortality (AUC 0.70), AKI, stroke, myocardial infarction.
  VIS outperformed APACHE II and SAPS II.

- Tohme et al., Interdiscip Cardiovasc Thorac Surg 2023 (DOI:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/icvts/ivad055): VIS in first 24h post heart
  transplantation independently predicts 1-year mortality.

- Sun et al. meta-analysis 2024 (DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-023-02403-1):
  58 surgical studies, VIS predicts mortality OR 1.08/unit (i.e., dose-response),
  AKI OR 1.26, prolonged ventilation OR 5.20.

**Verdict:** The core finding — "vasopressor load in the first 24h grades mortality
in a dose-response fashion" — is KNOWN, named, meta-analysed, and specifically
called out as a risk-stratification tool in surgical and post-surgical ICU settings.
An editor at Anesthesiology will know VIS immediately and will ask: "What does this
add to VIS?" If the paper does not address VIS explicitly and head-on, it will be
desk-rejected for ignoring an established prior art body.

**Severity: CRITICAL — ALREADY DISCLOSED (not in dossier as a named prior art).**

---

**Vasopressor dose-response mortality in sepsis: the Roberts et al. (2020) study.**

Roberts RJ et al., Crit Care Med 2020 (DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1097/CCM.0000000000004476): prospective multicenter cohort
(n=616, 33 sites), vasopressor dosing intensity defined in NOREPINEPHRINE
EQUIVALENTS (NEE — exactly the same construct as Finding 4), first 24h NEE →
30-day in-hospital mortality. Result: every 10 µg/min increase in NEE dose →
adjusted OR 1.33 [1.16, 1.53] for mortality. The paper also examines titration
pattern (early-high/late-low better than sustained-high). This is a prospective
study with explicit NEE construction, dose-response mortality relationship, and
adjustment for APACHE II and fluid volume.

**Verdict:** Finding 4 (landmark NEE → subsequent death, OR 2.27) has a DIRECT
prospective precursor in sepsis using the same metric (NEE). The "norepinephrine
equivalents dose predicts mortality dose-response" claim is NOT novel in sepsis.
The present paper's contribution relative to Roberts is: (a) a much larger ICU
cohort (n~24k vs 616); (b) landmark design explicitly addressing reverse-causation;
(c) extension to a perioperative / intraoperative setting (INSPIRE); (d) the
intraoperative "requirement" framing. These are real differences but they are
incremental, not foundational.

**Severity: CRITICAL — PARTIALLY DISCLOSED (Roberts mentioned in dossier as
background but not foregrounded as the direct prior art competitor for Finding 4).**

---

**Cumulative intraoperative norepinephrine dose and AKI.**

Saugel et al., BJA 2025 (DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2024.11.005): n=38,338
noncardiac surgery patients, cumulative intraoperative norepinephrine dose
independently predicts AKI (OR 1.02 per µg/kg, p<0.001), adjusting for
intraoperative hypotension. Published in BJA, directly relevant to Finding 2.
Ariyarathna et al., Ren Fail 2022 (DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/0886022X.2022.2061997):
cumulative intraoperative vasopressor dose independently predicts AKI in elderly
noncardiac surgery (adjusted OR 2.47).

**Verdict:** The cumulative intraoperative vasopressor dose → AKI link (Finding 2)
has been published by major groups in large cohorts. Finding 2 is not novel as a
primary claim; it is confirmatory/additive.

**Severity: CRITICAL for Finding 2 (not the primary claim, but the overlap is
direct). MODERATE for the primary paper if Finding 2 is positioned as secondary.**

---

**Intraoperative vasopressor dose and morbidity/mortality (pancreatic surgery).**

Dietrich et al., Langenbecks Arch Surg 2025 (DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00423-025-03669-w): n=525 pancreatoduodenectomy patients,
maximum norepinephrine dose → composite outcome (mortality + fistula +
pancreatectomy), dose-response U-shaped. This is a single surgical cohort, not
large-scale, but shows the perioperative vasopressor dose → outcome frame is active.

**Severity: MODERATE — prior art exists for the intraoperative dose → outcome
frame, though at smaller scale and different procedure.**

---

**Closed-loop vasopressor literature and the implicit control-theory premise.**

Joosten et al., BJA 2020 (DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2020.08.051) and
Chiou et al., J Clin Anesth 2026 (DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinane.2026.112230,
systematic review): closed-loop vasopressor (CLV) systems explicitly treat the
anesthesiologist as a feedback controller titrating vasopressor to a MAP target.
This is the engineering-control framing applied to vasopressor titration. The
concept that the clinician functions as a MAP-regulating feedback controller is
IMPLICIT in every closed-loop vasopressor paper; however, none of these papers
draws the logical conclusion that the MAP is therefore the controlled (and less
informative) variable and the DOSE is the informative signal.

**Verdict:** The control-theory PREMISE is implicit in the closed-loop vasopressor
literature but the INFERENTIAL STEP — "therefore measure dose not MAP, because MAP
is regulated to a set-point" — is NOT made explicitly in the literature found. This
is the most defensibly novel element of the paper.

**Severity: MODERATE — the premise is known, the inferential step is novel. Must
be stated carefully.**

### 1B. What IS genuinely novel — NEW vs KNOWN

| Element | Status | Novelty Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| VIS / NEE load predicts mortality dose-response | KNOWN | 2019–2024 meta-analysis, 58 studies |
| NEE in sepsis predicts mortality (Roberts 2020) | KNOWN | Direct prior art for Finding 4 |
| Cumulative intraop vasopressor → AKI | KNOWN | Saugel 2025, n=38k in BJA |
| Control-theory PREMISE (clinician = feedback controller) | IMPLICIT in CL-vasopressor lit | Not explicitly applied as here |
| Control-theory INFERENTIAL STEP: measure dose not MAP because MAP is regulated | **GENUINELY NEW** | No paper found making this explicit argument with empirical support (MAP CV vs dose CV) |
| Vasopressor requirement as a PATIENT TRAIT (reliability: test-retest, split-half) | **GENUINELY NEW** | Reliability/ICC framing not found in VIS/NEE literature |
| Split-half reliability (0.82/0.87) within the same surgical case | **GENUINELY NEW** | VIS uses max or mean in first 24h; no split-half reliability characterization found |
| Cross-case / cross-procedure trait stability (INSPIRE cross-ops 0.32) | **NEW** | Not found in VIS lit |
| Phenylephrine replication (pure α1) | **NEW** | Useful construct validation not found |
| Landmark prospective test defeating reverse-causation (alive at 24h → subsequent death) | **NEW in design** | Roberts 2020 is prospective but contemporaneous; landmark framing is original |
| Mortality beyond lactate+SOFA labs (severity-adjusted OR 2.4–2.5) | **INCREMENTAL** | Roberts adjusts for APACHE II; the present paper adds more granular severity control |
| Negative-control exposure (propofol OR 0.88 vs norepi OR 3.01) | **GENUINELY NEW** | Novel falsification design; not found in this context |
| Concordance null (no decision-benefit) | **NEW (negative result)** | Honest, important, unusual to publish |

**Summary of the genuine novelty core:**

Three things are genuinely new that the VIS/NEE literature has not done:
1. The formal control-theory argument with empirical data (MAP CV 0.09 vs dose CV 0.44).
2. The TRAIT characterization — split-half and test-retest reliability framing, treating vasopressor requirement as a stable patient phenotype rather than a severity score element.
3. The landmark prospective design explicitly defeating reverse-causation at scale.

The VIS literature uses vasopressor load as a prognostic score element; it does not
frame it as a phenotypic trait with test-retest reliability, nor does it apply the
control-theory argument to justify why the dose is more informative than the
pressure. The reliability quantification (ICC/split-half) is the single most
differentiating element from prior art: it is the logical bridge from "dose grades
mortality" (known) to "dose reflects a stable patient physiology" (new).

## 2. CLINICAL MEANING — what does a clinician DO with a "reliable trait" when concordance is null?

**CRITICAL / NEW (not fully addressed in dossier)**

This is the hardest editorial challenge. The paper asserts risk-stratification
value but explicitly shows no decision-benefit (concordance null, actionability
tests null). An Anesthesiology editor will ask:

> "If I cannot act on this — if knowing a patient's vasopressor requirement does
> not change my management and does not improve outcomes — what is the clinical
> purpose? Is this a physiological curiosity or a paper?"

Three possible answers, in increasing order of editorial persuasiveness:

**Answer A (weak): Pure characterization / physiology.** "We describe a new
physiological construct." This is publishable in a specialty journal (e.g., BJA,
Anesthesia & Analgesia) but not at the Anesthesiology tier without a stronger
clinical hook.

**Answer B (moderate): Research tool / patient stratification for trials.** "The
requirement trait enables enrichment: enroll high-requirement patients into
vasopressin trials, MAP-target trials, renal-protection trials." This is a real
use case but must be made explicit and quantified (e.g., what is the NNT to
identify one high-risk patient?).

**Answer C (strongest — and the one the paper should make): The concordance null
IS the finding.** "Clinicians think they are managing hemodynamic risk by targeting
MAP. The control-theory argument predicts that if you titrate pressor to MAP, your
patient's MAP tells you nothing about their risk — the dose does. The concordance
null confirms this: acting on information that is hidden in the MAP (but encoded in
the dose) does not improve outcomes because the information is not currently
extracted and acted upon. The finding does not prescribe an action; it identifies a
latent variable that current practice is blind to. The actionable implication is:
future decision tools should incorporate dose-requirement, not just MAP."

This reframing positions the null as mechanistic evidence supporting the
control-theory argument, not as a weakness. It also sets up a prospective
intervention (e.g., dose-informed trigger for vasopressin add-on) that the paper
can call for without claiming to have demonstrated.

**Severity of the clinical-meaning gap: CRITICAL if unaddressed; MODERATE if
Answer C is made explicit.**

## 3. POPULATION GENERALIZABILITY

**MODERATE — already partially disclosed, needs sharper statement in the abstract**

The study population is arterial-line/already-on-pressor. This is approximately:
- 15–25% of all surgical patients in centers with high arterial line rates
- Specifically the highest-risk surgical stratum

The limitation is real: "vasopressor requirement" cannot be measured in patients
who never receive vasopressors. The paper cannot claim to risk-stratify the
general surgical population; it characterizes a physiological signal WITHIN the
vasopressor-receiving population. This is not fatal — it is an honest scope — but
the primary claim language must reflect it. "The per-kg requirement is a reliable
trait" should read "...within patients receiving vasopressor support." The abstract
should state this limitation in the first paragraph, not the discussion.

Secondary concern: VitalDB is a South Korean single-center cohort (Seoul National
University Hospital). The intraoperative hemodynamic management patterns (MAP
targets, pressor thresholds) may differ from US/European practice in ways that
affect the CV ratio argument. This must be stated.

**Severity: MODERATE — already partially disclosed; needs front-loading.**

## 4. THE SINGLE BIGGEST DESK-REJECT RISK AND THE TOP REFRAMING LEVER

### 4A. Biggest desk-reject risk (single sentence)

> **The paper will be desk-rejected at Anesthesiology if it fails to
> differentiate itself explicitly and head-on from the VIS literature (which
> already established that vasopressor load in the first 24h predicts surgical
> mortality in a dose-response fashion across 58 studies and 29,920 patients)
> and from Roberts et al. 2020 (which already reported prospective NEE
> dose-response mortality in ICU sepsis), without explaining what the reliability/
> trait-framing and control-theory argument add beyond those prior bodies.**

An editor who knows the VIS literature (and any cardiac anesthesia editor will)
reads the abstract — "vasopressor load predicts mortality, dose-response" — and
sends the form-letter: "This finding is established. Please see VIS literature."

### 4B. Top reframing lever (the one change that most raises the tier)

**Reframe the entire paper around RELIABILITY as a novel biological construct, not
around dose-response prediction.**

The VIS literature asks: "Does vasopressor load correlate with outcome?" (yes,
known). The present paper answers a different question that the VIS literature
never asked: "Is the vasopressor requirement a STABLE PATIENT PHENOTYPE — a trait
that reflects patient physiology rather than acute severity or titration
practice?"

The split-half reliability (0.82 within the same operation), the phenylephrine
replication (0.87, independent drug), the MIMIC ICU ICC (0.95), and the INSPIRE
cross-procedure stability (0.32) answer this question in a way the VIS literature
never has. VIS is a contemporaneous severity score; the present paper argues the
requirement is a patient trait. That is a conceptually distinct claim.

**Concrete reframing for the title and first paragraph:**

Current implicit framing: "Vasopressor dose predicts mortality."
Target framing: "The vasopressor requirement is a stable patient physiological
trait — the control-theory argument explains why, and the reliability data prove
it — and this trait grades mortality beyond severity."

The control-theory section (MAP CV vs dose CV) is not a methods curiosity; it is
the mechanistic RATIONALE for why reliability is possible at all (if MAP were
unregulated, within-case variability would prevent a stable per-epoch dose metric).
This mechanistic chain — regulate MAP → dose encodes patient physiology →
dose is reliable → reliable dose grades mortality — is the novel intellectual
contribution that VIS did not make and that the paper should lead with.

**Implementation:** The abstract should open not with "vasopressor dose predicts
mortality" but with "Vasopressor requirement is a stable, reliable physiological
trait in patients receiving intraoperative/ICU vasopressor support, explicable by
feedback regulation of arterial pressure." The VIS comparison should appear in the
introduction as a deliberate contrast: "VIS established that load predicts
outcomes; we establish that requirement is a trait."

| JAMA / NEJM | No | No clinical action demonstrated; population too restricted |

**Realistic target:** Anesthesiology IF the paper is restructured to lead with the
trait-reliability / control-theory novelty and explicitly contrast with VIS.
BJA is the floor. Crit Care Med is a plausible alternative venue for the ICU arm.

## 7. PRIOR ART VERDICT SUMMARY

**KNOWN (substantially pre-empted):**
- Vasopressor load (VIS, NEE) predicts surgical/ICU mortality dose-response: KNOWN
  since 2010 (VIS invention), meta-analysed in 2024 (58 studies).
- NEE prospectively predicts septic shock mortality: Roberts 2020, n=616.
- Cumulative intraop norepinephrine → AKI: Saugel BJA 2025, n=38k.
- Closed-loop MAP regulation as control-theory framing: implicit in CLV literature
  since 2010.

**GENUINELY NEW (what the paper uniquely contributes):**
- Explicit control-theory argument with empirical support (MAP CV 0.09 vs dose
  CV 0.44) justifying the dose as the informative signal.
- Reliability characterization of vasopressor requirement as a PATIENT TRAIT
  (split-half 0.82/0.87, ICC 0.95, cross-procedure 0.32): not found in VIS/NEE
  literature, which measures load without characterizing its reliability as a
  phenotype.
- Landmark prospective design at scale (n=24k) defeating reverse-causation: new
  in design, not done in the VIS or Roberts literature.
- Negative-control exposure (propofol) as falsification: novel.
- Concordance null as a mechanistic finding (current practice is blind to the
  trait): new framing.

**Bottom line for the editor:** The paper's intellectual core is the reliability /
trait / control-theory package, not the dose-mortality association. The dose-mortality
association is the validation that the trait matters, not the finding itself. If the
paper is submitted with dose-mortality as the headline, it will be rejected as
redundant with VIS. If submitted with trait-reliability as the headline and VIS
addressed explicitly, it is competitive for Anesthesiology.
