- **This is the single biggest assumption.** Without L2 queue data we cannot measure it; a full sensitivity table over phi is below so the result can be repriced.

## BLUNT VERDICT

- **How much of the +0.12/ct edge do we actually capture?** At the realistic operating point (p=0.2, phi=0.35), the realized captured edge is **+0.0481/ct** -- about **40.0% of the +0.12/ct** headline. The gap is structural: to fill at all you rest near the band floor, which trades per-contract edge for fill probability; and 74% of markets never fill.

- **Honest deployable $/week:** at a small bankroll ($50-500) the realistic capture is roughly **$28.53/week** (~$500 bankroll). Even removing the bankroll constraint entirely, the realistic-phi **capacity ceiling is only ~$1235.15/week** -- the edge is bound by retail longshot-buy VOLUME, not by capital. Larger size does NOT scale past that ceiling; it just leaves orders unfilled.

- **Where capacity binds:** per-market fill saturates by ~50-250 shares in most markets (median fillable market is tiny); the aggregate saturates at the ceiling above. This is a **breadth** game (many tiny fills), not a size game.

- **The result hinges on phi**, which we cannot measure without L2 queue data. The sensitivity table spans phi=0.10..1.00; a realistic seller sharing the book earns tens of $/week, a lone dedicated maker low-hundreds. Treat any single number as an order-of-magnitude, not a point estimate.

- **Bottom line:** the edge is REAL but the fill-adjusted, capacity-bound, honest number is a **small-niche low-hundreds-of-$/week at best**, and materially less at a starter bankroll or in a crowded book. This is a legitimate paper-forward maker sleeve, not a capital-absorbing strategy.
