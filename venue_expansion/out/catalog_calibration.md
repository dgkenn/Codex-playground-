# Catalog calibration: is the executable price ever off the realised frequency by more than the fee?

**VERDICT: INSUFFICIENT** (frozen PASS/KILL rule applied verbatim; section 8)   |   **Anchor (a) replication control: REPLICATED**

Spec CATALOG_CALIBRATION_SPEC.md, registered 2026-10-29. 280 comparisons; Bonferroni two-sided alpha = 1.786e-04; gate z = 3.7475 (99.982% Wilson). Seed 20261029.

## 0. Plain reading (written after the run; every number is in the tables below)

**Anchor (a) REPLICATED, so the pipeline is believable.** Re-deriving TAIL_PRICING's T=15 sell-at-bid table from its 503 cached hours through this module's code reproduces all 7 longshot buckets exactly (n and YES count, and the 46.6% no-bid fraction). On a fresh, independently fetched KXBTCD stratum (302 hourly events), 5 of 5 sell-direction longshot cells have the mean bid inside the Wilson interval (95% and 99.98%). Caveat: T=60 is nearly empty for KXBTCD because 98% of hourly strikes open at or after close - 60 minutes, so the fresh stratum effectively tests T=15 only.

**Verdict: INSUFFICIENT (frozen rule, verbatim). It is power-bound and, for KILL, structurally unreachable. It is not a hit and it is not a clean catalog-wide KILL.**

* **PASS: 0 cells.** No cell's 99.982% Wilson interval clears price + fee in the profitable direction, on the literal, event-adjusted or day-adjusted interval, at any n. Of 280 cells, 29 have a positive point-estimate edge (a zero-drift null would give about 140; the drift is the spread plus the fee); only one of those has n >= 100 (Financials, T=15, sell, 95-98c: n=192 from 24 events on 17 days, f=94.3% against bid 97.1c, edge +1.9c, literal interval [84.5%, 98.0%] against a clearing bar of 96.1%, event-adjusted [47%, 99.7%]). Nothing squeaked past anything. With 280 comparisons and intervals this wide, a cell that did clear would still have needed the event-adjusted check and a replication before it meant anything.
* **Only 1 of 280 cells reaches the criterion-2 floor** (n >= 1000 priced, >= 100 events, >= 20 days): Crypto, T=15, BUY, 1-2c (n=32,900, 364 events, 60 days). It is the dead tail: asks of about 1c, mostly with no bid, and 1.4% non-zero entry volume, realised f = 0.015% [0.003%, 0.07%] against a 1.01c ask. The interval excludes the price, so the frozen KILL clause fails, but it excludes it in the UNPROFITABLE direction (the buyer overpays; the seller has no bid to hit). That is the 1c tick floor, not exploitable mispricing.
* **Why the sample is thin.** 2,169 events were priced (2,082 from the live API, 87 from `/historical`), about 3 hours of API-limited fetching (HTTP 429 on roughly one call in four). Coverage: Sports 0.16% of 165,070 events, Politics 38.0% of 703, Financials 1.78% of 15,023, Economics 11.2% of 2,377, Climate and Weather 2.80% of 9,544, Commodities 0.83% of 31,905, Crypto ex-KXBTCD 0.35% of 76,905, KXBTCD 21.5% of 1,404. Every stream is an exact prefix of its saved shuffle. Median cells need 1,100 (Economics) to 10,300 (Politics) events to reach n = 1000; Politics' whole live universe is 703 events, so criterion 2 is unreachable there at any sample size.
* **The calibration curves are the deliverable and they say the same thing in every category.** Executable-price buckets are conditioned on one side of a book that is frequently wide, one-sided or stale, so f lands INSIDE the quoted spread and never beyond it. BUY cells: f sits far below the ask (Sports T=15: ask 71c -> f 33%, ask 85c -> 53%, ask 96c -> 32%; Economics: ask 70c -> 13%, ask 49c -> 17%). SELL cells: f sits far above the bid (Sports: bid 49c -> 67%, bid 67c -> 89%; Economics: bid 14c -> 53%, bid 28c -> 80%). 85 of 280 cells have a literal interval that excludes the executable price (79 of the 199 cells with n >= 30), and every one of them is in the unprofitable direction; zero cells, at any n, exclude the price in the profitable direction. Pooled over buckets, the taker's fee-inclusive edge per contract is negative with a CI excluding zero in 25 of the 28 category x T x direction rows (e.g. Sports T=15: buy -19.7c, sell -8.8c; Economics T=15: buy -20.0c, sell -19.4c); the other three (Politics T=60 buy, Crypto T=60 buy and sell; n = 741, 552, 172) span zero, the best point estimate being +0.75c (Crypto T=60 sell, 16 events), and no row has a CI above zero.
* **A cause worth naming: stale one-sided books.** Many high-ask buy cells are resting offers on markets already determined NO (for example about 25 NHL first-goal-scorer markets in one game with an ask of 95c, no bid, zero entry volume, entry candle about two hours old). They are executable on paper, lose, and carry no information about calibration. Restricting to entries where BOTH sides were quoted (section 7b-ii) shrinks but does not remove the pattern: Sports T=15 buy -14.5c net, sell -8.2c net.
* **What this does and does not close.** Within the resolution of this sample (cell half-widths of roughly 10-30 percentage points outside Crypto), there is no corner of Kalshi where the executable price is off the realised frequency by more than the fee in the taker's favour. It cannot rule out an edge of a few cents in any single cell; it can only say none is large, and the pooled rows say none is common. It does not establish the venue-wide fairness KILL the spec hoped for, because the frozen KILL clause ("interval contains the price in both directions") fails in thin books on spread alone. A KILL clause written on the fee-clearing direction (no cell clears price +/- fee) would read as KILL on these numbers (0 of 280 clear, 0 of 1 floor cell) but would still rest on intervals too wide to be called a venue-wide null; that reading is descriptive and was NOT the frozen rule.
* **Contradictions with the spec's premises.** (1) The catalog has 15,416 series, 9,116 in the seven categories (Sports 4,055), not 9,191 / 4,067. (2) Elections (2,331 series), Entertainment, Mentions and others are separate catalog categories and are outside the frozen seven; Politics has only 703 settled events in the live window. (3) 98% of KXBTCD strike-markets (54,144 of 55,205) open at or after close - 60 min, so T=60 can barely be measured on the replication control. (4) An empty ask side is printed as 1.0000 (100c), not 0, so it is classed as untradeable. (5) The literal Wilson interval treats markets as independent; the event design effect reaches 2.6 in the one floor cell and the two-way sports partitions are perfectly anti-correlated. (6) Criterion 2 and the KILL clause cannot both be met in most of the catalog at any feasible API volume.

## 3. Anchor (b): calibration curves (the deliverable, whatever the verdict)

Per category x direction x T x frozen bucket. `f` = realised YES frequency (official `result`). `px` = mean executable price (ask for buy, bid for sell, cents). `edge` = cents per contract before sizing: buy `100f - ask - fee`, sell `bid - fee - 100f` (descriptive; there is no EV t-gate). `W99.98` = the gate's Wilson interval; `evt-adj` = same interval widened by the event design effect. `gate` = per-bar flags `P` (fee-clearing on the literal interval) / `N` (n>=1000, >=100 events, >=20 days) / `U` (untradeable <= 70%) / `V` (>=50% nonzero entry volume) / `C` (no event >5%, no day >25%). `vol<=` = summed entry-candle volume, an upper bound only.
